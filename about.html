About South Brunswick Aerospace Club

Aerospace Education starts with curiosity.

Founded in 2023 by Aarush Modali and Zafir Shamsi, South Brunswick Aerospace began with a simple idea: make rocket science and STEM exciting through hands-on work.

Our story

Built one challenge at a time.

From a first competition team to rocketry, research, and NASA-supported experiments.

2023–24

Starting with a first launch

Aarush and Zafir, two Boy Scouts with a passion for aerospace engineering, recruited Aman, Havish, Shyam, and Kevin for the club’s first American Rocketry Challenge team. With a steep learning curve and a limited budget, the team reached ARC Nationals.

2024–25

Growing the team

The team expanded to 10 members, with Zafir Shamsi as captain and Aarush Latti as vice captain, and returned to nationals. The club’s executive board included its founding members.

2025–26

Two teams, new frontiers

The club grew to two teams of 10. Zafir Shamsi and Aarush Latti served as club president and vice president, and as Team A’s captain and vice captain. Team B was led by Vivek Banker and Ritwik Desai.

What we do

Students learn by designing, building, and flying.

The South Brunswick Aerospace Club is a student-led nonprofit organization dedicated to fostering interest and excellence in aerospace engineering and related fields.

We bring math and physics to life with projects that students can see, test, and improve.

01 / FLIGHT

Rocketry & aviation

Design, build, and fly model rockets, airplanes, and drones. Members also use a flight simulator, and some are learning to fly real airplanes.

02 / HARDWARE

Electronics & fabrication

Build flight computers and avionics, work with electronics, and bring designs into the real world with CAD and 3D printing.

03 / CHALLENGES

Competitions & launches

Compete in the American Rocketry Challenge, attend regional launches through TRA/NAR clubs, and take on NASA design challenges.

04 / RESEARCH

Experiments & analysis

NASA TechRise gave Team A $1,500 and NASA/Future Engineers mentorship to build a stratospheric payload for “NDVI Mapping via Machine Learning.”

FIELD NOTE

After the payload’s flight box crash-landed and much of its data could not be recovered, Zafir Shamsi led a summer machine-learning research project, “CanyonBench-Trace: Testing Whether Aerial Vision-Language Models Answer from the Pixels.” The team submitted the work to NeurIPS.

Our goal

Make every student fall in love with science and engineering.

Many of our students have come from South Brunswick High School, which has supported competition applications. To make operations easier and reach more students, Zafir Shamsi established the organization as a nonprofit.
